Additional pattern from the comments

Many suggestions were about making play more dynamic or clearer.

Across the written feedback, reviewers repeatedly asked for stronger gameplay loops or clearer pacing: more copyright questions, a real opponent/multiplayer mode, slower or clearer questions, better instructions, more ways to stay engaged, and more predictable interaction. The suggestions are different game-to-game, but they point toward the same next-step design question: how can the learning goal become part of what the player actually does?

What’s next

Keep the polish — move the learning into the game loop.

The reviews suggest a clear next round: preserve the strong visual and technical execution, while making the copyright teaching, challenge, and player choices feel inseparable from the gameplay.

1. Build teaching into playUse copyright decisions, questions, or evidence as the mechanic — not just a layer added on top.
2. Playtest for clarity + engagementLook closely at instructions, pacing, challenge, controls, and what keeps players wanting another turn.
3. Document AI clearlyLink the documentation, name the tools, and explain what was AI-made versus student-made.
